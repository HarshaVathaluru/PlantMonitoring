### 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This document defines the operational response requirements, priority classifications, threshold mappings, handover protocols, and fault management rules for all automated alarms generated by the plant-wide SCADA and machine monitoring systems at Vindhya Precision Works. This procedure is mandatory for all maintenance technicians, shift supervisors, and production machine operators.

---

### 2. ALARM PRIORITY CLASSIFICATIONS AND RESPONSE TIMES
All alarms shall be categorized into four distinct priority levels (P1 through P4). Every priority level carries a mandatory maximum response time, defined as the interval between initial system annunciation and the arrival of qualified maintenance personnel at the affected asset control panel or physical location.

*   **2.1 Priority 1 (P1) – Critical Safety / Imminent Failure**
    *   *Response Time:* Immediate. Maximum allowable response time is **3 minutes**.
    *   *Action:* Requires immediate machine trip or manual emergency stop execution, followed by diagnostic assessment by the on-duty Senior Maintenance Technician.

*   **2.2 Priority 2 (P2) – Major Process Deviation / Equipment Damage Risk**
    *   *Response Time:* Maximum allowable response time is **15 minutes**.
    *   *Action:* Requires operator intervention or technician investigation to prevent escalation to a P1 condition.

*   **2.3 Priority 3 (P3) – Minor Fault / Performance Degradation**
    *   *Response Time:* Maximum allowable response time is **60 minutes**.
    *   *Action:* Requires logging and troubleshooting during the current operating shift.

*   **2.4 Priority 4 (P4) – Informational / Maintenance Advisory**
    *   *Response Time:* Maximum allowable response time is **4 hours** during standard shifts, or deferred to the next scheduled maintenance window for unmanned off-shifts.
    *   *Action:* Non-urgent status notification requiring administrative log verification.

### 3. SENSOR PARAMETER THRESHOLD MAPPING
The control system shall assign alarm priorities based on real-time telemetry from four primary sensor modalities: Vibration (Velocity/Acceleration), Temperature, Pressure, and Motor Current. The thresholds mapped to priorities P1 through P4 are defined in Table 3.1.

**Table 3.1: Parameter-to-Priority Mapping Matrix**

| Sensor Modality | Priority 1 (P1) | Priority 2 (P2) | Priority 3 (P3) | Priority 4 (P4) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vibration** (Overall Velocity / ISO 10816 Class) | $\ge 11.2\text{ mm/s RMS}$ | $\ge 7.1\text{ mm/s RMS}$ | $\ge 4.5\text{ mm/s RMS}$ | $\ge 2.8\text{ mm/s RMS}$ |
| **Temperature** (Bearing / Winding) | $\ge 95^\circ\text{C}$ | $\ge 85^\circ\text{C}$ | $\ge 75^\circ\text{C}$ | $\ge 65^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Pressure** (Hydraulic / Pneumatic / Lube) | $< 40\text{ bar}$ or $> 220\text{ bar}$ | $< 60\text{ bar}$ or $> 200\text{ bar}$ | $< 80\text{ bar}$ or $> 180\text{ bar}$ | Deviation $> 10\%$ from nominal setpoint |
| **Current** (Motor Load / Amperage) | $\ge 125\%$ Full Load Amps (FLA) for $> 5\text{s}$ | $\ge 115\%$ FLA for $> 30\text{s}$ | $\ge 105\%$ FLA for $> 120\text{s}$ | Unbalanced phase current $> 7\%$ |

*Exception 3.1.1:* Hydraulic Press Line 4 (Assets HP-401 through HP-408) operates under high-transient pressure profiles; for these specific assets, P1 pressure high threshold is mapped at $> 245\text{ bar}$ and P1 pressure low is mapped at $< 30\text{ bar}$.

### 4. SHIFT-HANDOVER REQUIREMENT FOR OPEN ALARMS
*   4.1 The outgoing shift maintenance supervisor and the incoming shift maintenance supervisor shall conduct a mandatory physical and digital handover audit at the central maintenance dispatch console **15 minutes prior** to the official shift changeover hour (06:45, 14:45, and 22:45 daily).
*   4.2 All unacknowledged or active P1, P2, P3, and P4 alarms remaining in the system log at the time of handover must be explicitly documented in the Shift Handover Ledger (Form SHL-99).
*   4.3 The incoming Lead Maintenance Engineer, Anil Deshmukh, or the designated acting shift lead, must digitally countersign all open P1 and P2 alarms before the outgoing shift personnel are cleared to leave the plant perimeter.

### 5. SUSPECTED FAULTY SENSOR MANAGEMENT
*   5.1 Any sensor exhibiting erratic outputs, signal freezing, loop-power loss ($< 4\text{ mA}$ on a $4-20\text{ mA}$ loop), or displaying values physically impossible for the process conditions shall be designated as a "Suspected Faulty Sensor."
*   5.2 **Mandatory Restriction:** A suspected-faulty sensor **may never be suppressed, bypassed, or masked in the SCADA/PLC configuration without an active maintenance work order** generated in the Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS).
*   5.3 When a sensor is suspected to be faulty:
    1. The technician shall raise a corrective work order with priority classification P3 within **30 minutes** of discovery.
    2. The technician shall apply a physical lock-out tag to the local junction box referencing the work order number.
    3. Operational safety shall be maintained by utilizing secondary physical verification (e.g., handheld infrared pyrometer for temperature, analog test gauges for pressure) at intervals not exceeding **2 hours** until the faulty sensor is calibrated, repaired, or replaced under the executed work order.
