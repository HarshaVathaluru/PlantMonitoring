# Preventive Maintenance Planning Standard

**VPW-SOP-ENG-041: PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE PLANNING AND SCHEDULING**
**Vindhya Precision Works — Maintenance Engineering Department**
**Effective Date:** 14 January 2026  
**Author:** R. Nambiar, Maintenance Engineering Lead  
**Approvals:** M. Sen (Plant Director), K. Joshi (Reliability Committee Chair)

---

### 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
1.1 This standard establishes the mandatory procedures for preventive maintenance (PM) planning, asset criticality classification, condition-monitoring conversion, backlog management, and task prioritisation at Vindhya Precision Works.  
1.2 Compliance with this document is mandatory for all maintenance planners, reliability engineers, and lead technicians across the manufacturing plant.

---

### 2. OPERATIONAL TIMELINE AND SHIFT PATTERNS
2.1 Maintenance planning and execution shall align strictly with the plant's continuous three-shift operating schedule:
*   **Shift A:** 06:00 to 14:00 hours (Duration: 8.0 hours)
*   **Shift B:** 14:00 to 22:00 hours (Duration: 8.0 hours)
*   **Shift C:** 22:00 to 06:00 hours (Duration: 8.0 hours)

2.2 Daily maintenance coordination meetings shall occur at 06:30, 14:30, and 22:30 hours to review shift handovers and immediate schedule adjustments.

---

### 3. ASSET CRITICALITY CLASSIFICATION
3.1 Every plant asset listed in the Vindhya Precision Works Asset Register shall be assigned a criticality class of A, B, or C based on three independent evaluation factors: Safety Risk, Environmental Impact, and Production Downtime Cost.  
3.2 An asset shall take the most severe criticality class indicated by any single one of the three factors. For example, if an asset has a Safety Risk of LOW (Class C) and an Environmental Impact of MINOR (Class C), but its downtime cost is 35,000 rupees per hour (Class A), the asset shall be designated as Class A.  
3.3 The production downtime-cost factor shall be calculated using the specific hourly downtime rate assigned to the individual asset within the asset register.

**Table 3.1: Vindhya Precision Works Asset Criticality Matrix**

| Class | Safety Risk | Environmental Impact | Production Downtime Cost (INR/hour) | Mandatory Inspection Interval |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Class A** | HIGH | CRITICAL | $\ge$ 30,000 | 7 days |
| **Class B** | MEDIUM | MAJOR | 10,000 to 29,999 | 30 days |
| **Class C** | LOW | MINOR | $<$ 10,000 | 90 days |

---

### 4. WORK ORDER ASSIGNMENT AND TRADE COMPETENCY
4.1 A maintenance work order may only be assigned to a technician who holds the verified trade competency required for the specific asset intervention.  
4.2 Permitted maintenance trades at Vindhya Precision Works are restricted to:
1. Mechanical
2. Electrical
3. Instrumentation
4. Hydraulics
5. Robotics

4.3 Cross-trade assignments are strictly prohibited unless the technician holds dual certification registered in the plant Human Resources and Engineering skills matrix database.

---

### 5. CONDITION-MONITORING ALERT CONVERSION
5.1 Any condition-monitoring alert generated by online sensors, portable vibration analysis, thermography, or oil sampling that exceeds the baseline warning threshold by $\ge 15\%$ shall be automatically converted into an unassigned corrective work order by the Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) within 120 minutes of alert generation.  
5.2 The reliability engineer shall review all converted alerts within 4.0 hours of creation to append specific fault codes and required spare parts lists.

---

### 6. BACKLOG THRESHOLD AND ESCALATION
6.1 The maintenance backlog shall be measured weekly every Monday at 08:00 hours using the Maintenance Work Order Backlog Index (MWOBI), expressed in total scheduled man-hours versus available technician man-hours.  
6.2 If the total plant maintenance backlog exceeds **240 hours** of uncompleted work, or if any single Class A asset work order remains unexecuted past its scheduled window by more than **24 hours**, the maintenance planner shall immediately escalate the condition to the Maintenance Engineering Lead and Plant Director via automated CMMS priority notification.  
6.3 Upon escalation, overtime shifts (Shift A-OT, Shift B-OT, or Shift C-OT) shall be authorized automatically by the Lead to reduce backlog below the 240-hour threshold within 72 hours.

---

### 7. PLANNING HORIZON
7.1 The maintenance planning horizon at Vindhya Precision Works operates on a rolling **12-week schedule window**, divided as follows:
*   **Weeks 1 to 2:** Locked schedule. No modifications permitted except for emergency breakdowns or severe condition-monitoring alerts.
*   **Weeks 3 to 6:** Frozen for material staging and contractor coordination. Minor sequencing adjustments permitted.
*   **Weeks 7 to 12:** Open planning window for baseline preventive maintenance generation and resource leveling.

---

### 8. TASK PRIORITISATION: PREDICTIVE ALERTS VS. SCHEDULED TASKS
8.1 When a predictive maintenance (PdM) alert conflicts with a routine scheduled preventive maintenance (PM) task for resource allocation or equipment availability, priority shall be assigned according to the following strict hierarchy:
1. **Priority 1:** Emergency breakdowns (Unplanned corrective).
2. **Priority 2:** Predictive maintenance alerts originating from Class A assets or critical condition-monitoring threshold breaches ($\ge 25\%$ above baseline).
3. **Priority 3:** Routine scheduled Class A preventive maintenance tasks.
4. **Priority 4:** Predictive maintenance alerts originating from Class B and C assets.
5. **Priority 5:** Routine scheduled Class B and Class C preventive maintenance tasks.

8.2 A predictive alert (Priority 2) shall pre-empt and displace a scheduled Class C preventive task (Priority 5) without requiring Plant Director approval, provided the displacement does not breach statutory safety regulations. Displacement of a Class A scheduled PM (Priority 3) by a predictive alert of lower criticality is prohibited.
