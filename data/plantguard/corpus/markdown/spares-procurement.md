# Spare Parts and Procurement Policy

**Document ID:** VPW-SOP-MNT-042  
**Revision:** 4.1  
**Effective Date:** 15 January 2026  
**Owner:** Maintenance Engineering Lead, Vindhya Precision Works  
**Applicability:** All Plant Maintenance Technicians, Stores Personnel, and Procurement Officers  

---

### 1. Purpose and Scope
1.1 This Standard Operating Procedure defines the inventory management, spare parts stock thresholds, reorder methodologies, supplier classification tiers, purchase authorisation workflows, and obsolescence review cadences for all mechanical, electrical, and pneumatic assets at Vindhya Precision Works.

1.2 Adherence to this document is mandatory for all maintenance engineering and stores personnel. Any deviation requires written authorisation from the Plant Head.

---

### 2. Criticality-Based Minimum Stock Rules
2.1 All inventory items stored in the Vindhya Precision Works central stores must be assigned a criticality code within the Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system based on their operational impact.

2.2 Criticality levels and their corresponding minimum stock rules are defined as follows:

*   **Class A (Critical Asset Protection):** Parts essential for continuous plant operation or safety-critical systems where failure halts primary manufacturing lines. 
    *   *Minimum Stock Rule:* Safety Stock ($SS$) must equal 100% of maximum expected consumption during the maximum vendor lead time, plus a standing buffer of 2 units. Minimum stock shall never be set below 2 units.
*   **Class B (Essential Operations):** Parts supporting secondary systems or sub-assemblies where failure reduces production capacity by no more than 30% and redundancy exists.
    *   *Minimum Stock Rule:* Safety Stock must equal 50% of maximum expected consumption during the vendor lead time, with a minimum stock floor of 1 unit.
*   **Class C (General Consumables and Non-Critical Hardware):** Standard fasteners, common O-rings, and auxiliary shop supplies where failure causes zero production downtime.
    *   *Minimum Stock Rule:* Reorder is triggered strictly on a fixed minimum-maximum basis. Safety stock is set to 0 units; reorder point equals expected consumption during lead time.

---

### 3. Reorder Point Calculation and Worked Example
3.1 The Reorder Point ($ROP$) for all inventory items must be calculated using the following formula within the ERP system:

$$ROP = (\text{Average Daily Consumption} \times \text{Lead Time in Days}) + \text{Safety Stock}$$

3.2 **Worked Example (Vindhya Precision Works Part ID: VPW-MNT-8812, Hydraulic Servo Valve):**
*   **Asset Criticality:** Class A
*   **Average Daily Consumption ($ADC$):** 0.25 units per day (equivalent to 7.5 units per 30-day month)
*   **Lead Time ($LT$):** 14 days (retrieved from the ERP inventory record for this specific part)
*   **Safety Stock ($SS$):** Calculated as maximum consumption during lead time (0.5 units/day $\times$ 14 days = 7 units) plus the mandatory Class A buffer of 2 units. $SS = 9$ units.

**Calculation:**
$$\text{Lead Time Demand} = 0.25 \times 14 = 3.5\text{ units}$$
$$ROP = 3.5 + 9 = 12.5\text{ units}$$

3.3 Fractional values resulting from the $ROP$ calculation must be rounded up to the nearest whole integer in the ERP system. Therefore, the system reorder point for Part ID VPW-MNT-8812 is **13 units**. When on-hand plus on-order stock drops to or below 13 units, a purchase requisition is automatically generated.

### 4. Approved Supplier Tiers
4.1 Vindhya Precision Works recognises three approved supplier tiers for spare parts procurement. Supplier tier classification must be maintained in the master vendor database.

*   **T1 (OEM or Authorised Distributor):** Original Equipment Manufacturers or direct suppliers holding official certification from the equipment maker. Required for all Class A critical spares.
*   **T2 (Approved Alternate):** Secondary manufacturers or aftermarket suppliers who have passed the Vindhya Precision Works Quality Assurance audit and metallurgical testing protocols. Permitted for Class B and Class C spares.
*   **T3 (Local or Spot Supplier):** Unaudited local vendors utilised strictly for emergency, off-catalog, or immediate spot-purchase operational requirements. Restricted to Class C items or non-standard hardware.

4.2 **Lead Time Assignment:** Lead times are not fixed by supplier tier. The exact procurement lead time (ranging from 2 to 90 days) is quoted per individual part number and stored directly within the ERP inventory record. Procurement officers must use the specific part-level lead time rather than a generic tier average when planning replenishment schedules.

### 5. Purchase Authorisation Thresholds and Automated Controls
5.1 Purchase orders generated by the ERP system or submitted by the maintenance engineering team are governed by strict financial thresholds based on total order value in Indian Rupees (INR).

5.2 Authorisation tiers are enforced programmatically by the ERP system as follows:

*   **Up to 200,000 INR:** The reorder system may raise and approve the purchase order automatically, provided the requisition matches a pre-approved stock replenishment rule and uses a T1 or T2 supplier.
*   **Above 200,000 INR and up to 1,000,000 INR:** Requires mandatory electronic approval by the Maintenance Manager (currently assigned to Shri. R. K. Sharma) prior to purchase order transmission.
*   **Above 1,000,000 INR:** Requires mandatory electronic approval by the Plant Head (currently assigned to Dr. A. V. Deshmukh) prior to purchase order transmission.

5.3 **Named Exception and Automation Constraint:** No purchase order with a total value exceeding 200,000 INR may be raised or executed automatically by the ERP system, regardless of stock criticality or automated reorder triggers. All requisitions exceeding 200,000 INR must pause in the ERP workflow queue for manual intervention and sign-off by the designated authority outlined in Section 5.2.

### 6. Obsolescence Review Cadence
6.1 To prevent capital lock-up and avoid holding unserviceable inventory, the Maintenance Engineering Lead shall conduct a formal obsolescence review of all active stores items.

6.2 The obsolescence review must be executed strictly on a **semi-annual cadence** (every six months, completed by the 15th of June and the 15th of December of each calendar year).

6.3 During the obsolescence review, the ERP system shall generate an inactive inventory report detailing all parts with zero consumption over the preceding 365 days. 

6.4 The Maintenance Engineering Lead, in coordination with the stores supervisor, must evaluate each inactive part to determine if the parent machinery has been decommissioned. Parts identified as obsolete due to asset retirement must be flagged for scrap disposal or vendor buyback in accordance with Vindhya Precision Works financial disposal policies, and their maximum stock limits must be zeroed out in the ERP.
